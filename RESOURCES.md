## Data

### SDA: Survey Documentation and Analysis (UC Berkeley)

- **Link:** https://sda.berkeley.edu/ (datasets: https://sda.berkeley.edu/archive.htm)
- **What it is:** Free browser-based analysis of national survey data. No login, no software.
- **Datasets:** General Social Survey 1972–2024, American National Election Studies 1948–2024, Cooperative Election Study, Survey of Consumer Finances 1989–2025, Census microdata.
- **Tools:** Codebooks, frequencies, crosstabs, means, regression, charts, CSV export.
- **AP Research use:** A secondary-data method path. Public, de-identified data means no human participants, so the risk screen is "none" and no consent forms are needed. The exported CSV becomes the student's data file for analysis.
- **Cautions:** The interface is dated; use the SDA YouTube tutorials. Variable names are cryptic, so students read the codebook first. Created variables sit in a shared workspace, so name them distinctively.
- **Classroom use:** `classroom/sda-lab.html` is a one-period lab with a tested demo table.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-09. Demo table and year filter re-run 2026-11-11.

### Pew Research Center datasets

- **Link:** https://www.pewresearch.org/datasets/
- **What it is:** Pew's raw survey data, released some time after each report: American Trends Panel waves, global attitudes surveys, the Religious Landscape Study, and more.
- **AP Research use:** A secondary-data path like SDA, with no human participants. Students can pair a Pew report, as a source, with its raw data, as their own analysis.
- **Cautions:** Downloads need a free Pew account. Pew's terms state no minimum age, so a teacher account is the safer route. The terms limit reuse to excerpts, require Pew's citation and its disclaimer that Pew is not responsible for the student's analysis, and forbid trying to identify respondents. Files come in SPSS format, which needs converting for Sheets.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11; terms read 2026-11-12

### General Social Survey home site (NORC)

- **Link:** https://gss.norc.org/
- **What it is:** The survey's own site, run by NORC at the University of Chicago: data files, questionnaires, documentation, and a bibliography of studies that used the survey.
- **AP Research use:** The source of record for anything a student analyzes through SDA. The questionnaires show exactly how each question was asked. The bibliography helps a literature review.
- **Cautions:** The home page gives no citation format. Students cite the dataset in the class style, with the release and the date used. Its GSS Data Explorer tool was unreachable when checked.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-12

### Google Dataset Search

- **Link:** https://datasetsearch.research.google.com/
- **What it is:** A Google search engine for datasets across the web. Searching works without signing in.
- **AP Research use:** Finding whether data on a topic already exists, before choosing between collecting data and using public data.
- **Cautions:** It finds data; it doesn't vouch for it. Students judge each dataset's source, sample, and date like any other source.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11

### IPUMS USA

- **Link:** https://usa.ipums.org/usa/
- **What it is:** U.S. census microdata from the University of Minnesota, made consistent across years: censuses from 1790 to 2010 and the American Community Survey from 2000 on. The site says data and services are free of charge.
- **AP Research use:** Public data on households, work, language, and migration, down to local areas. A no-participants path like SDA.
- **Cautions:** Free registration is required before any download. The form asks for name, institution, and a research statement, and states no minimum age, so a teacher account is the safer route. Extracts are large and technical. SDA's census files are the easier start.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11; registration page read 2026-11-12. Approved by Mr. Portela 2026-11-11, with the cautions above.

### CDC WONDER

- **Link:** https://wonder.cdc.gov/
- **What it is:** The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's query system for public health data: births, deaths, cancer, environment, population, and more.
- **AP Research use:** Counts and rates for health topics, by year, place, age, and sex. Public data, no participants.
- **Cautions:** Whether any dataset needs a login was not confirmed. Small counts are suppressed, so local questions may return blanks. Students must record the exact query they ran.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11 (home page only). Approved by Mr. Portela 2026-11-11, with the cautions above.

## Methods texts (free and citable)

### Bhattacherjee, *Social Science Research: Principles, Methods, and Practices* (2012)

- **Link:** https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/oa_textbooks/3/
- **What it is:** A free open textbook from the University of South Florida covering the whole research process. License: CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. The full PDF downloads without a login.
- **AP Research use:** A citable methods text for justifying a method in the academic paper. It is the free stand-in for SAGE Research Methods.
- **Cautions:** Written for graduate students, so it reads densely; assign chapters, not the book. Dated 2012, so pair it with newer sources on online surveys.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11

### OpenIntro Statistics

- **Link:** https://www.openintro.org/book/os/
- **What it is:** A free introductory statistics textbook. The PDF downloads free, with an optional contribution. The publisher recommends it for college courses and self-study.
- **AP Research use:** A citable reference when a student reports a mean, a comparison, or a correlation and must explain the test.
- **Cautions:** Edition, authors, and license were not shown on the page checked (the download name suggests a 4th edition); confirm before citing. College level, so assign sections.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11. Approved by Mr. Portela 2026-11-11, with the cautions above.

## Free tools

Approved by Mr. Portela on 2026-11-11, with the cautions below. Read College Board's AI guidance first. None of these three uses AI to write or analyze for the student.

### ZoteroBib

- **Link:** https://zbib.org/
- **What it is:** A free, ad-free bibliography builder from the team behind Zotero. No account and no install. It supports APA, MLA, Chicago, and thousands of other styles.
- **AP Research use:** Building the reference list in the class style from a title, link, or DOI.
- **Cautions:** The list is stored in the browser on that device, not on a server. On a shared iPad it can be lost or seen by the next user, so students export or copy it out each session. Students still check every entry against the style sheet.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11

### Taguette

- **Link:** https://www.taguette.org/
- **What it is:** A free, open-source tool for qualitative coding. Import documents, highlight passages, and tag them. It exports the codebook, the tagged quotes, and the highlighted documents.
- **AP Research use:** Coding interview notes or open-ended survey answers. The exported codebook and quote list become part of the student's data file.
- **Cautions:** It installs on macOS, Windows, or Linux, which keeps data on that computer. The hosted version asks users to register, and its page states no privacy terms or storage location. Use the installed version for anything from participants. No iPad app was confirmed.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11; hosted page read 2026-11-12

### jamovi

- **Link:** https://www.jamovi.org/
- **What it is:** Free, open-source statistics software that looks like a spreadsheet. The desktop version is free and works offline. A cloud version runs in a browser, and the site says it works on Chromebooks and tablets.
- **AP Research use:** Descriptive statistics and simple tests on a student's own data file, with the output saved beside the data.
- **Cautions:** The cloud version has a free Guest plan that needs a sign-up but no payment card; it has queues, shorter sessions, and smaller data limits than the paid plan. iPad support is not named. Coded data only: no names in any file uploaded to the cloud. The software runs the test; the student still has to justify it.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-11; cloud plans read 2026-11-12

## Ethics and consent

### UC Berkeley CPHS informed consent templates

- **Link:** https://cphs.berkeley.edu/informedconsent.html
- **What it is:** Free Word templates and guidance for consent, parent permission, and child assent, with bracketed instructions and filled-in examples.
- **Most useful:** Assent form for ages 13–17 (school-based), parent permission form (school-based), online survey consent, interview with audio recording, consent checklist PDF, IRB decision tree PDF, AI consent language guide.
- **AP Research use:** Models for the ethics review packet's guardian consent and student assent forms. The Room 63 versions are in `classroom/consent-templates.docx`.
- **Cautions:** Remove UC Berkeley branding, the CPHS protocol number, and Berkeley contacts. A form must never suggest Berkeley approved a study. The school's own review route decides; these are models only.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-09

## Background reading (not citable in the paper)

### Litmaps: "Types of research methods"

- **Link:** https://www.litmaps.com/articles/types-of-research-methods
- **What it is:** A free blog article by Mina Zarei, dated 2025-04-29, on the Litmaps company site. It sorts methods into qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods, gives each technique's best use, advantages, limits, and a made-up example, then a five-step checklist for choosing a method.
- **AP Research use:** A plain-language first overview before students choose a method. Good for a mini-lesson or a sort activity.
- **Cautions:** It cites no sources and is company marketing, so students must not cite it to justify a method in the paper. They cite a methods text instead. The page's table of contents is broken.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-09

### ResearchRabbit infographic: "How to Write a Research Methodology Section"

- **Source:** An image from ResearchRabbit, a literature-mapping tool. Shared by Mr. Portela on 2026-11-09; original web address not recorded.
- **What it is:** Six steps: restate the problem, choose a paradigm (positivist, interpretivist, pragmatic), describe the methods, justify the choices, address reliability and validity, and acknowledge limitations. Each step has a one-line example.
- **AP Research use:** A one-page checklist for drafting and peer-reviewing the method section. Step 4, "justify your choices," matches what the rubric rewards most.
- **Cautions:** No sources, so not citable. Its examples, such as 300 surveyed students, are larger than most AP Research samples. "Paradigm" may need a short explanation. The step 5 split is slightly off: triangulation belongs to both kinds of research, not only quantitative.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-09

### Sticky-note planning grid: "Choosing the research methods and planning the measurement"

- **Source:** An image shared by Mr. Portela on 2026-11-09; creator and web address unknown. It looks like it comes from a program-evaluation guide.
- **What it is:** A grid that plans two methods side by side, a feedback questionnaire and one-to-one interviews. Each is planned by Why, What, When, Who, and After gathering data.
- **AP Research use:** A blank version makes a strong method-planning template. It shows how to justify each method, as in "interviews over focus groups because the questions are personal," and how a mixed design connects: interviews explain the survey numbers.
- **Cautions:** Its example studies bullying victims, a sensitive topic with minors. That needs administrator approval under the ethics review packet and is a poor model for students. Its 3-month and 6-month follow-ups don't fit the AP Research calendar. Not citable.
- **Checked:** 2026-11-09

### SAGE Research Methods

- **Link:** https://methods.sagepub.com/
- **What it is:** A subscription library on how to do research: method guides, case studies of real projects, datasets for practice, and videos. Its Project Planner and Methods Map walk a researcher from question to method.
- **Likely AP Research use:** Choosing and justifying a method, and writing the method section with scholarly support.
- **Blocker:** The site redirects to a SAGE sign-in. Access needs an institutional login (school, college, or public library). The Room 63 student access route is unknown.
- **Public pieces (no login):** Sage's guide to the database, https://sagepub.libguides.com/research-methods, lists its collections and the most popular methods. The Sage Research Methods Community blog, https://researchmethodscommunity.sagepub.com/, is also public. Every link into the database itself, including the Methods Map, goes to the sign-in page.
