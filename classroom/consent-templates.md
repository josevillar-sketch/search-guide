**For the student researcher. Do not hand this page to participants.**

1. Do not use any form until Mr. Portela has approved your proposal and your instruments. College Board requires the teacher to review every proposal, survey, and set of interview questions before any data is collected. A study with more than minimal risk cannot be approved.
2. Attach your draft forms, with your survey or interview questions, to item 9 of your Inquiry Proposal Form.
3. If you plan to publish, enter a competition, or present your findings publicly outside this course, tell Mr. Portela now. College Board then requires approval from a registered institutional review board as well.
4. Pick the forms your study needs. A participant under 18 needs **two**: the guardian permission form and the student assent form. An adult needs the adult consent form. An online survey uses the survey opening text as its first screen.
5. Replace every [bracket] with your own details, then delete the brackets. Keep each form to one page.
6. Give each family the language they read best. The English and Spanish forms say the same thing.
7. Use plain words. If a ninth grader would not understand a sentence, rewrite it.
8. Keep signed forms with your Process and Reflection Portfolio. College Board lists permissions among the documents it must hold. Never post or share them.

These templates follow the structure of the public consent templates from the University of California, Berkeley, Committee for Protection of Human Subjects. They are a starting point. Mater Academy's review decides what your study needs.

# Guardian permission form

**Mater Academy · AP Research student project**

**Study title:** [title of your study]
**Student researcher:** [your first and last name], grade [11 or 12]
**Teacher:** Mr. Jose Portela, AP Research, Room 63

Dear parent or guardian,

I am a student in the AP Research course at Mater Academy. I am doing a research study about [topic, in one plain sentence]. I am asking your permission for your child to take part.

**What your child will do.** [One or two sentences: for example, answer a paper questionnaire of 12 questions.] It will take about [number] minutes, [where and when].

**Risks.** [The risks from your risk screen, in plain words. If the risk is minimal, say: I do not expect any risk beyond those of an ordinary school day.] Your child may skip any question.

**Benefits.** There is no direct benefit to your child. The study may help [who or what] understand [what].

**Privacy.** I will not use your child's name. Each answer is stored under a code number. Only my teacher and I will see the answers. I will delete them by [date]. [If you record audio: I will record the interview only with your permission and your child's, and delete the recording by (date).]

**It is voluntary.** Taking part is your choice and your child's choice. Saying no will not affect your child's grades or standing at school in any way. Your child may stop at any time. [If the study happens during class: A child who does not take part will do (alternate activity) for the same amount of time.]

**Questions.** Please contact Mr. Portela at [school email] or the school at [school phone].

This study was reviewed for Mater Academy by [Mr. Jose Portela, AP Research teacher] on [date].

# Student assent form (ages 13 to 17)

**Mater Academy · AP Research student project**

**Study title:** [title of your study]
**Student researcher:** [your first and last name], grade [11 or 12]

My name is [first name]. I am a student in AP Research, and I am doing a study about [topic, in one plain sentence]. I would like to ask you to be in it.

**What is a research study?** It is a careful way to find something out by collecting information. This study is not schoolwork for you, and you will not be graded on it.

**Why am I asking you?** [One sentence: for example, because you are in grades 9 to 12 at this school.]

**What will you do?** [One or two sentences.] It will take about [number] minutes.

**Could anything bad happen?** [Plain words from your risk screen. If minimal: I do not expect anything worse than an ordinary school day.] You can skip any question you do not want to answer.

**Who will know what you said?** I will not use your name. Your answers get a code number. Only my teacher and I will see them, and I will delete them by [date].

**Do you have to do this?** No. It is your choice. Nobody will be upset if you say no, and it will not change your grades. You can say yes now and change your mind later. Just tell me you want to stop. Your parent or guardian also has to give permission, but even if they say yes, you can still say no.

**Questions?** Ask me any time, or ask Mr. Portela in Room 63.

☐ Yes, I want to be in this study.  ☐ No, I do not want to be in this study.
[If recording:] ☐ You may record my voice.  ☐ Please do not record me.

# Online survey opening screen

Use this as the first screen of the survey. The participant must choose "I agree" before any question appears. For participants under 18, a signed guardian permission form is still required first.

**[Title of your study]**

I am a student in the AP Research course at Mater Academy. This survey is part of my research study about [topic, in one plain sentence].

- **What you will do:** answer [number] questions. It takes about [number] minutes.
- **Voluntary:** you may skip any question or close the survey at any time. Saying no will not affect your grades or standing.
- **Privacy:** the survey does not ask for your name. [If the tool collects email addresses, turn that setting off, and say so here.] I will delete the answers by [date].
- **Risks:** [plain words from your risk screen].
- **Questions:** contact my teacher, Mr. Portela, at [school email].

This study was reviewed for Mater Academy by [Mr. Jose Portela, AP Research teacher] on [date].

☐ I have read this, and I agree to take part.  ☐ I do not want to take part. [This choice ends the survey.]
