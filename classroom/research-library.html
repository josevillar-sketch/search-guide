Research Library

Every source and tool here was opened and checked. Each card says what it is for, whether you may cite it, and what to watch out for. To find sources on your own topic, use your Search Guide slip.

Nothing here writes or changes your research question.Your question is the one in your log. These tools help you answer it.

Find data that already exists

  • Cite it in your papersda.berkeley.edu

    SDA: Survey Documentation and Analysis

    UC Berkeley

    National survey data you can analyze in your browser. No login. Includes the General Social Survey since 1972.

    Mind this. Read the codebook first. You can only use the questions that were asked.

  • Cite it in your papergss.norc.org

    General Social Survey

    NORC at the University of Chicago

    The survey's own site: the exact questionnaires and a list of studies that used it.

    Mind this. Use it to see how each question was worded.

  • Tooldatasetsearch.research.google.com

    Google Dataset Search

    Google

    A search engine for datasets. Use it to find out whether data on your topic already exists.

    Mind this. It finds data. You still judge who collected it, from whom, and when.

  • Ask Mr. Portela firstpewresearch.org

    Pew Research Center datasets

    Pew Research Center

    Raw data behind Pew's national surveys.

    Mind this. Downloading needs an account, and Pew's terms limit how the data is reused. Ask first.

  • Ask Mr. Portela firstusa.ipums.org

    IPUMS USA

    University of Minnesota

    Census data on households, work, language, and migration.

    Mind this. Needs a registered account, and the files are large. SDA's census files are the easier start.

  • Cite it in your paperwonder.cdc.gov

    CDC WONDER

    Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

    Public health counts and rates: births, deaths, and more, by year, place, age, and sex.

    Mind this. Small local counts are hidden. Write down the exact query you ran.

Choose and justify a method

  • Cite it in your paperdigitalcommons.usf.edu

    Social Science Research: Principles, Methods, and Practices

    Anol Bhattacherjee, University of South Florida

    A free textbook on the whole research process. Use it to justify the method you chose.

    Mind this. Written for graduate students. Read the chapter you need, not the whole book.

  • Cite it in your paperopenintro.org

    OpenIntro Statistics

    OpenIntro

    A free statistics textbook. Use it when you must explain a mean, a comparison, or a correlation.

    Mind this. College level. Check the edition on the title page before you cite it.

  • Read it, do not cite itlitmaps.com

    Types of research methods

    Litmaps blog

    A plain overview of qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods.

    Mind this. A company blog with no sources. Use it to get oriented, then cite a real methods text.

Study people safely

  • Ask Mr. Portela firstask in Room 63

    Room 63 consent and assent forms

    Mr. Portela

    The forms for a guardian's permission, a student's own agreement, and adult consent, in English and Spanish.

    Mind this. No form goes out, and no data is collected, until your proposal and questions are approved.

  • Read it, do not cite itcphs.berkeley.edu

    Informed consent templates and guidance

    UC Berkeley

    What a real university consent form contains, with a checklist.

    Mind this. Models only. A form must never suggest that Berkeley approved your study.

Analyze what you collected

  • Tooljamovi.org

    jamovi

    jamovi project

    Free statistics software that works like a spreadsheet.

    Mind this. Use coded data only: no names in any file you upload. You still have to justify the test you run.

  • Tooltaguette.org

    Taguette

    Taguette project

    A free tool for coding interviews and open-ended answers: highlight a passage, give it a tag.

    Mind this. Use the installed version, not the web version, for anything from participants.

Cite it

  • Toolzbib.org

    ZoteroBib

    Zotero

    A free bibliography builder. No account. Paste a title or link, pick the class style.

    Mind this. It saves only on that device. Copy your list out before you leave a shared iPad. Check every entry.

Do not use

PapersOwl and sites like it. Their advice pages sell papers written for you. College Board requires your paper to be your own work.
