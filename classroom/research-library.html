B

Find data that already exists

Drawer B · 6 cards

  • R63.B.01Cite it

    SDA: Survey Documentation and Analysis

    UC Berkeley

    National survey data you can analyze in your browser. No login. Includes the General Social Survey since 1972.

    Mind thisRead the codebook first. You can only use the questions that were asked.

    Open sda.berkeley.edu ↗
  • R63.B.02Cite it

    General Social Survey

    NORC at the University of Chicago

    The survey's own site: the exact questionnaires and a list of studies that used it.

    Mind thisUse it to see how each question was worded.

    Open gss.norc.org ↗
  • R63.B.03Tool

    Google Dataset Search

    Google

    A search engine for datasets. Use it to find out whether data on your topic already exists.

    Mind thisIt finds data. You still judge who collected it, from whom, and when.

    Open datasetsearch.research.google.com ↗
  • R63.B.04Ask first

    Pew Research Center datasets

    Pew Research Center

    Raw data behind Pew's national surveys.

    Mind thisDownloading needs an account, and Pew's terms limit how the data is reused. Ask first.

    Open pewresearch.org ↗
  • R63.B.05Ask first

    IPUMS USA

    University of Minnesota

    Census data on households, work, language, and migration.

    Mind thisNeeds a registered account, and the files are large. SDA's census files are the easier start.

    Open usa.ipums.org ↗
  • R63.B.06Cite it

    CDC WONDER

    Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

    Public health counts and rates: births, deaths, and more, by year, place, age, and sex.

    Mind thisSmall local counts are hidden. Write down the exact query you ran.

    Open wonder.cdc.gov ↗
C

Choose and justify a method

Drawer C · 3 cards

  • R63.C.01Cite it

    Social Science Research: Principles, Methods, and Practices

    Anol Bhattacherjee, University of South Florida

    A free textbook on the whole research process. Use it to justify the method you chose.

    Mind thisWritten for graduate students. Read the chapter you need, not the whole book.

    Open digitalcommons.usf.edu ↗
  • R63.C.02Cite it

    OpenIntro Statistics

    OpenIntro

    A free statistics textbook. Use it when you must explain a mean, a comparison, or a correlation.

    Mind thisCollege level. Check the edition on the title page before you cite it.

    Open openintro.org ↗
  • R63.C.03Don't cite

    Types of research methods

    Litmaps blog

    A plain overview of qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods.

    Mind thisA company blog with no sources. Use it to get oriented, then cite a real methods text.

    Open litmaps.com ↗

Drawer E · 2 cards

  • R63.E.01Tool

    jamovi

    jamovi project

    Free statistics software that works like a spreadsheet.

    Mind thisUse coded data only: no names in any file you upload. You still have to justify the test you run.

    Open jamovi.org ↗
  • R63.E.02Tool

    Taguette

    Taguette project

    A free tool for coding interviews and open-ended answers: highlight a passage, give it a tag.

    Mind thisUse the installed version, not the web version, for anything from participants.

    Open taguette.org ↗
