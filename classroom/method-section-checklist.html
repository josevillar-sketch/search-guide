Method section checklist

Writer's codeReader's codeDate
What College Board asks of this section: it "explains and provides justification for the chosen method, process, or approach and its alignment with the research question." Two verbs, explain and justify, and one test, alignment.

Explain: could a reader repeat it?

Who or what. Who or what was studied, how many, and how they were chosen.
Instrument. The survey, interview guide, dataset, or texts are described. The full instrument is in an appendix.
Steps. What was done, in order, with dates and how long each part took.
Analysis. How the data was analyzed, named plainly: counts, a comparison, coding for themes.

Ethics: is it on the page?

Approval. Says the study was approved before any data was collected.
Consent and privacy. Says how people agreed to take part and how their identities are protected. For public data: says no participants were contacted.

Justify: why this, and not something else?

Alignment. One or two sentences connect the method to the research question in the writer's own words.
The road not taken. Names at least one other method and gives a reason it was not used.
A methods source. Cites a methods text or a published study for the choice. A blog or infographic does not count.
Limits. Names what this method cannot show. Small sample, one school, one year, self-report.

Evidence: can it be traced?

Every number. Each number in the section matches the writer's own data file.
Nothing borrowed. No number, example, or wording comes from a sample paper or a template.

Reader: one place I could not follow (ask a question; do not rewrite it)

Writer: what I will change, in my own words

Readers ask questions and point to gaps. They do not write, rewrite, or suggest wording. The paper is the writer's own work.
